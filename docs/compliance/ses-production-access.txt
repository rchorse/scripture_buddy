VERIFIED IDENTITY
We have a verified domain identity, not just an address: scripturebuddy.net,
verified in us-west-2 with DKIM signing enabled (2048-bit), a custom MAIL FROM
subdomain (mail.scripturebuddy.net) for SPF alignment, and a DMARC record
published. All sending is from noreply@scripturebuddy.net.

WHAT THE APPLICATION IS
ScriptureBuddy (https://scripturebuddy.net) is a scripture-study app for
families. It has no chat or messaging of any kind, no advertising, and no
mailing list. We do not send marketing, newsletters, or bulk mail of any
description, and we have no facility to do so.

HOW OFTEN WE SEND
Every message is triggered by an action the recipient just took in the app.
There is no scheduled or campaign sending. Current volume is a handful of
messages per day during private testing. We expect under 100 per day at first
and well under 1,000 per day at realistic scale. Peak sending is one message per
account creation.

HOW RECIPIENT LISTS ARE MAINTAINED
There is no list. We do not maintain, buy, rent, import, or scrape recipient
lists. An address enters our system in exactly one way: a person types their own
address into our sign-up form to create their own account. It is stored on that
account in AWS Cognito and used only for that account's own transactional mail.
There is no way for one user to cause mail to be sent to a different person's
address. When an account is deleted, the address is erased with it.

THE THREE MESSAGE TYPES

1. Parental consent confirmation (our main use case)
   When an adult creates an account for a child under 13, the US Children's
   Online Privacy Protection Act requires verifiable parental consent before the
   child's account may be used. We email the parent — at the address on the
   parent's own account, which they entered themselves — a one-time link to
   confirm. A second confirmatory message follows 24 hours later, which is the
   "email plus" method the FTC describes. The child's account is unusable until
   the parent confirms.

   Subject: Please confirm consent for <child-username> on ScriptureBuddy
   Body (abridged):
     You asked to create a ScriptureBuddy account for <child-username>.
     Because they are under 13, US law (COPPA) requires us to confirm that a
     parent or guardian consents before the account can be used.
     You are being asked to consent to let them <purpose>.
     What we collect for a child account: a username you choose, their birth
     date (used only to apply the right protections), their timezone, and their
     progress in the app. We do not collect an email address, phone number, real
     name, or location for a child. We do not show ads, and we never sell or
     share their information.
     [one-time confirmation link, valid 72 hours]
     If you did not ask for this, ignore this email and nothing will happen.

2. Account verification codes at sign-up — a numeric code sent to the address
   the person just entered, to prove they own it.

3. Password reset codes — sent only when the account holder requests a reset.

BOUNCES, COMPLAINTS AND UNSUBSCRIBES
- We monitor the account's bounce and complaint rates and will configure an SNS
  event destination for bounce and complaint notifications before production
  sending.
- Hard bounces are suppressed immediately and the address is not retried. In our
  case a hard bounce means a broken account rather than a stale list entry, so
  it is a defect we investigate rather than a row we quietly drop.
- Complaints are treated as an immediate, permanent suppression for that
  address.
- We rely on the account-level suppression list in addition to our own.
- Unsubscribe does not apply: these are consent, verification, and password
  messages that exist because the recipient asked for them, and none of them are
  promotional. Any user can delete their account from within the app, which
  erases the address and stops all mail to them permanently.

WHY WE NEED PRODUCTION ACCESS
In the sandbox we can only deliver to addresses verified in advance. The parental
consent flow sends to whichever address a parent used to create their own
account, so it cannot work in the sandbox for anyone but us — which means the
COPPA consent step, and therefore any account for a child under 13, cannot
function.
